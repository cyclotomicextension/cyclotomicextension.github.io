<p>My name is Additi Pandey. I am a PhD candidate in the Division of Applied Mathematics at Brown University. I am interested in learned models that preserve the underlying physical structure, so that long trajectories stay stable and reproduce the correct statistics. I have previously worked in number theory and in formalising mathematics in the Lean theorem prover (check out my repositories Formalising Mathematics Project <a href="https://github.com/cyclotomicextension/Formalising-Mathematics-Project-2">2</a>, <a href="https://github.com/cyclotomicextension/Formalising-Mathematics-Project-3">3</a> as well as <a href="https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2022/07/29/teaching-formalisation-to-mathematics-undergraduates/">this</a>). My Master's thesis, entitled <a href="https://github.com/cyclotomicextension/MSc-Thesis-Algebraic-Number-Theory-.git">"Quadratic & Cubic Reciprocity Laws"</a> delved into the intricacies of the quadratic and cubic reciprocity laws, as well as primes of the form x<sup>2</sup> + ny<sup>2</sup>.</p>

<p>I hold a ScM in Applied Mathematics from Brown University, an MSc in Pure Mathematics from Imperial College London, and a Bachelor of Science degree in Mathematics from Jesus and Mary College, University of Delhi. Additionally, I attended a graduate summer school at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research in 2021 and a summer school on AI and computer vision at the International Institute of Information Technology, Hyderabad, in 2023. My research experience encompasses work in the areas of algebraic number theory, epidemic modeling, geometry, and group theory, which I have conducted at institutions such as Imperial College London, IIT Kanpur, Delhi University, and the Harish Chandra Research Institute in Allahabad.</p>
